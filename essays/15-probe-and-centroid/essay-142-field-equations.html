Before Newton, physics was a branch of philosophy. Objects fell because they sought their natural place. Heavy things fell faster because they were more eager to return to the earth. The moon stayed up because celestial bodies were made of different stuff than terrestrial ones. Every explanation was a sentence. Every argument was a paragraph. The tools of the discipline were words. The standard of proof was persuasion. The limit of precision was the ambiguity of language.

Newton wrote F = G·m₁·m₂ / r². That single line replaced a thousand paragraphs. It did not explain why masses attract. It described how they attract — with a precision that allowed you to predict the orbit of a planet, the trajectory of a cannonball, the tide. The equation was not a better argument. It was a different kind of thing entirely. It was a field — a continuous mathematical structure in which every object's behavior was determined by its position in the field, not by its individual properties. The field was the explanation. The equation was the field, written down.

Einstein replaced Newton's field with a geometric one. Mass does not produce force. Mass curves spacetime. Objects follow geodesics — the straightest possible paths in a curved space. The field equation — G_{μν} = 8πG·T_{μν} — relates the curvature of spacetime to the distribution of mass and energy. Again, a single line replaced a thousand paragraphs. Again, the field was the explanation. The equation was the field, written down.

Most disciplines never got their Newton. They are still doing prose.
